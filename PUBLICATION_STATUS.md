**One correction carried in the repository.** The E4 condition was originally described as a parser-only ablation.
It is not: E4 was scored by a notebook-local evaluation loop with different tool schemas, different serialization,
its own tool implementations, and a run date two weeks later. The README and `docs/` describe it as
*adapter-fixed, with the parser and the evaluation client both changed*. A client-matched E4 re-run has not been
performed. Any text still calling it "parser-only" is out of date and should be corrected, not cited.

### What the results are, stated plainly

All four pre-registered contrasts are supported and the harm test is **not falsified** — which is a test the
mechanism could fail, not a fifth hypothesis it passed. Alongside them, three findings argue against the proposed
design and are reported with equal prominence: ungated enforcement scores 9.7 pp *higher* than the gated
mechanism at more than twice the harm; most of the benefit lies in the labels rather than the enforcement; and the
same gating driven by an LLM arbiter gives no gain and more harm. The best condition answers 61.3% of tasks
correctly. None of this has been externally reviewed.

### Known gaps, before anyone cites this

1. **The bibliography was not re-verified against publisher records** in the final preparation pass. The 37
   entries were checked earlier against arXiv metadata or title search; no entry was added afterwards.
2. **The literature search covered arXiv only** and closed on 14 November 2026, with most entries read at
   abstract level. The paper's novelty statement is explicitly bounded to "the work we examined".
3. **The evaluation set has no human validation.** It is synthetic, templated, English-only, model-written and
   model-annotated, and by construction favourable to a lexical arbiter. It is not presented as a benchmark of
   record.
4. **Author, affiliation, funding, acknowledgement and ethics fields are empty by design** in the manuscript.
   None was invented.
